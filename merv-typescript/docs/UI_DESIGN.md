# Merv UI design — "the lab notebook"

The browser UI is a research lab's notebook, not an admin console. A person opens it to answer three questions in this order: what needs me, what is running, what has been recorded. Everything else is configuration and stays out of the way until asked for.

This document is the contract for the 2026-09-16 redesign. `docs/UI_PLUGIN.md` still describes the plugin mechanics (rows, `ui.shell`, `ui.read`); nothing here changes those.

## Principles

1. **Few places.** The left rail lists places, not records. Four sections plus the project and Settings. Rows registered by plugins are reached from within their section, never from the rail.
2. **One thing to look at first.** Every page has a single dominant element: the title line. Secondary navigation sits inside that line; counts and status sit beside the labels they qualify. Nothing competes with the title.
3. **No chrome in normal use.** No section headers, disclosure triangles, refresh buttons, explanatory paragraphs under titles, eyebrow ids, or card borders around lists. Explanations live in empty states, where they help. Creation forms and heavy tables are collapsed behind one control.
4. **Configuration stays possible.** Settings keeps the plugin table, the session block and the introduction editor. Row pages keep their filters and forms. They are folded, not removed.
5. **Personality comes from structure, colour semantics and voice, not decoration or display type.** One clean sans throughout (the founder rejected the serif/typewritten direction on 2026-09-16); every record kind has one colour and one glyph, the colour on its kind label wherever the record appears and the glyph in the rail and in its empty state; mono only for identifiers. Copy is plain, short and written by a colleague, not a system.

## The rail

Width 224px, same background as the page, one soft line on the right. From top to bottom:

| Row      | What it is                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| wordmark | `merv` in the sans at 15px/600, with the hide-sidebar button at the right.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| project  | The project name in the sans, 15px/600. It is the link to the Overview (`/`). Accounts that may switch projects get a small switch icon that appears on hover and focus (always visible under 880px); it opens the existing project chooser.                                                                                                                                                                  |
| sections | One row per navigation section, in `SECTION_ORDER`: Research, Work, Agents, Feed, each with its section colour as an 8px dot before the label (the legend of the app). A dot appears only when a row in the section is degraded or unavailable, with the detail as its title. |

The active section is marked by a 2px accent bar at the left edge of the row and ink-coloured text; inactive rows are muted. Section labels: `research → Research`, `work → Work`, `operations → Agents`, `activity → Feed`. Unknown groups keep their humanised label after the known four.

## The title line

On a row's index route (`pathname === row.path`) the shell renders the page header. Views do not render their own title on index routes.

```
RESEARCH                                   ← eyebrow: section label, 11px, letter-spaced, muted
Tasks · Experiments 2 · Reviews · Reflections    ← sans 22px/600; active row in ink, siblings muted links at 22px/500; counts 13px beside their label
```

Siblings are the other registered rows of the same section, in row order. A section with one row shows just the title. The overview (`/`) uses the same grammar with the project name as its title and the introduction's first line beneath it. Detail routes (`/tasks/wf_…`) keep the view's own back link and record title at 22px/600, with the record's kind label above it.

Row-level actions (a "New cycle" button, a filter) render below the title line, right-aligned, at most one primary and one secondary control. Lists refresh themselves; there are no refresh buttons.

## Visual language (reference: problemma.ai canvas, 2026-09-16)

- **Type:** one sans, the system stack (`-apple-system, Inter, Segoe UI, Roboto, Helvetica, Arial`), 14px body, 13px meta, 11px uppercase labels with 0.06em tracking. Headings 22px/600, section headings 15px/600. No display face, no italics for emphasis, no serif anywhere. Mono only for ids and machine text. Numbers tabular.
- **Ground and surfaces:** page ground `#f7f8fa` (dark: `#111214`); records and panels are white surfaces `#ffffff` (dark: `#1a1b1e`) with a 1px `#e5e7eb` border (dark `#2a2b30`), 8px radius and a 1px shadow `0 1px 2px rgba(16,24,40,.04)`. Ink `#111827` / muted `#4b5563` / faint `#666d7b` (dark: `#e5e7eb` / `#adadb6` / `#8e8e98`); every ink that is read holds 4.5:1 on all three grounds, and the old pale faint survives only as `--ghost` for what is drawn and never read (rulings of 2026-09-20).
- **Kind colours** (one per record kind, worn by the uppercase kind label, the process diagram and the wash of an empty state; each theme states its own value as a `--kind-*` token, light / dark): research cycles and claims purple `#6d28d9` / `#a78bfa`; papers and records blue `#2563eb` / `#60a5fa`; tasks and experiments teal `#0f766e` / `#14b8a6`; reviews and reflections red `#c81e1e` / `#f87171`; proposals and consolidation amber `#b45309` / `#f59e0b`; agents, sessions, people and code slate `#475569` / `#94a3b8`; feed and artifacts gray `#5f6875` / `#a1a1aa`. Sections take the colour of their first kind: Research purple, Work teal, Agents slate, Feed gray. Semantic greens/ambers/reds for state stay as they are.
- **Record card:** a white surface with the kind edge; first line the uppercase kind label with its icon in the kind colour; then the title 14px/600 ink; then one or two lines of 13px muted; a footer line with the state dot and state word in 11px uppercase. Lists are stacks of cards with 8px gaps on the ground, or, for tables, one white surface holding a plain ruled table.
- **Accent:** `#2563eb` for the one primary control on a page (`--active-fill`, the same in both themes so white type stays legible on it), and `--active` for links on hover and focus rings, which lifts to `#60a5fa` on the dark ground; the current rail row is a soft pill, not the accent.
- **Chrome stays minimal:** the rail, one title line, one action row, then content. No canvas, no minimap, no legend panel — the one page that draws a graph is the experiment record's _How it got here_ section (`workflow.process`, rendered as a row of states and one lane per attempt), and the carve-out it gets is a legend, nothing more: still no canvas library, no minimap and nothing draggable. The reflection and consolidation pages render the same derivation as rungs of text, which needs no carve-out at all.
- **Where a page stands:** every page is the width beside the rail less one gutter on each side, the same on both sides (`--page-pad-x`: 40px, growing with the screen from 1100px to 96px; 16px on a phone), so a page is centred at any width with the rail open or hidden. The control a hidden rail leaves at the window's edge is cleared inside that gutter, never added to one side of it. Lists, tables, the map and the split pane fill the column (founder, 2026-09-17: the page uses the width of the screen); a block read as sentences keeps its own 62–68ch reading line and starts at the column's left edge. The two panes of a split keep the gutter at their outer edges, so a list's title stays where it was when a record opens beside it. A page that is one reading column and nothing else, the Agent's conversation, is that column centred in the page (founder, 2026-10-02: the Agent page was "left skewed"). Inside that column an answer, a card and a receipt are each as wide as the column, the same edges as the bar and the composer; only the person's own words stay a block at the right (founder, 2026-10-02: the output was “too narrow … messaging style”).

## Pages

- **Overview**: project name, one-line introduction, then three stacked blocks in fixed order: _Needs you_, _In motion_, _Recorded_. Plain lists, no cards. A block mounts only while its owning row is registered. (Phase B.)
- **Index pages**: title line, optional action row, then the list or table. An empty state is the kind's glyph, a few words and the control that makes the first record (rulings of 2026-09-20). Creation forms open from a single button and close after success.
- **Detail pages**: back link, kind label, record title, status beside it, then sections in reading order. Unchanged in structure.
- **Settings**: one place with rooms (Introduction, Members, Integrations, Keys, Connections, Plugins, Session) under one `Settings` title. The plugin table and the sidebar-row table are each a collapsed fold whose summary is its name, its count and one health pill.

## Where design effort goes

Minimal everywhere buys the right to be deliberate in four places. A 2026-09-16 design panel (four proposing lenses, three verifying judges scoring value, simplicity of use, feasibility and personality against the code and seeded data) ranked the candidates; these four won, in this order.

1. **The standing line (Overview).** One ordered list of what is not moving and whose move it is. Each line is a record card: kind label, the record name, one human sentence derived from the gate, who and when, and the one control; the server's own instruction is kept, folded, for the agent it was written for (rulings of 2026-09-20, which supersede the earlier "verbatim" wording). The order is policy and must stay small: a line is _yours_ when the blocker is `input_required` on a record whose producer is the signed-in actor, or when a review is unclaimed or claimed by you and still open; it is _an agent's_ when the blocker is a role restriction such as `forbidden`; it is _nobody's_ when the blocker is `dependencies_pending`. An unknown blocker code falls through to a neutral waiting line carrying the server's text; it is never promoted to "needs you". Tasks ship guidance inline in `task.list`; experiments and cycles use `workflow.status_and_next`, capped to the most recent eight. Dependency names come from `dependencies[].name`, never from the kind.
2. **The verdict page (`/reviews/:id`).** A criterion row reads as a sentence: the criterion, the producer's confirmation for the same check, the reviewer's finding, and the cited evidence readable in place. Controls come from `workflow.status_and_next` actions (`start_review` ready, `submit_review` blocked with the server's reason), never from browser-side rules. One primary control states its consequence in plain words. It replaces `ReviewCard` and the findings table wherever they appear.
3. **The feed as one narrative column.** Posts are paragraphs in their author's voice; a state change is one quiet line; anything a line is about is a name you can click. Activity folds into it: verdicts, state changes and agents joining earn a line, `artifact.created` and `feed.posted` never appear. The Activity row goes away.
4. **Claims beside what tests them.** The statement is the dominant element at 16px/600; the standing sits beneath; each testing experiment reduces to one plain sentence found by inverting `experiment.testedClaimIds`. `review.claimId` is a review lease, not a research claim, and is never joined. The tension between a human-set standing and machine evidence is shown, not resolved.

Deferred: the experiment record as an attempt spine (every experiment has one attempt today), the sessions floor, pending proposals in the paper.

## Chrome ruling of 2026-09-16 (late, after Tinker)

The founder pointed at Thinking Machines' Tinker console: "There is no chrome whatsoever, only lines there exist to separate things, things don't have borders. The color is too sterile, but we can handle that." This refines the visual language above; it does not replace it.

- Surfaces lose their borders. A 1px hairline in the border colour is the only divider, and only where two things meet; a list is rows separated by hairlines, not a stack of boxes.
- A box is drawn only where the box is the object: the property card and the record cards on the map, a guard that names what it is about to change, a criterion beside its evidence, the list a record picker opens over the page. Everything else sits directly on the ground.
- The kind colour and icon stay in the kind label, the kind icon, the kind dot on a rail row and the state word. They are what keeps the page from reading sterile.
- No left-edge accent anywhere (founder, 2026-09-17): no kind edge on records or map cards, no left rule on rounds, ladders, lanes or track steps (a quotation inside a rendered Markdown document keeps one hairline edge, the only way a quote reads as one), no inset bar on the rail's active row or the split pane's open row. A hairline between two panes is a divider, not this pattern.
- While loading, a list shows grey skeleton rows in its own grid; a total not yet known renders as an em dash, never a zero and never a spinner.
- Density comes from the type scale and the hairlines, never from borders, shadows or filled backgrounds.

## No identifiers in the UI (founder ruling, 2026-09-17)

The founder: "stop putting id's in UI, that is useless." No record, row, panel, card, feed line or evidence reference shows an identifier as text: no `Id` rows, no id chips, no `wf_`, `art_`, `review_` or `claim_` strings, no installation or repository numbers. A thing is named by its title, name or filename, and linked where a route exists. An id an author wrote into prose is read as the record's name and link; one that no list can name is cut to a short form (`art_…e959e2`) with the whole id in its hover title, because dropping it would change the author's sentence (rulings of 2026-09-20). Identifiers still live in routes, keys, hover titles and prefix search, where a person who pastes one is served without ever reading one.

## Verbs (wave 4)

The founder found the labels were not a vocabulary — `New claim`, `Start a reflection wave`, `Start consolidation`, `Write one`, `Claim this review`, `Enable dispatch`, `Halt every live lease`, `Advance when ready`, `Prepare download` — so a label never predicted what it powered. From this wave every control that acts reads `<Verb> <object>` in sentence case, from this table and nothing else. Never a sentence, and never a second phrasing of the same act. The opener and the form's heading read the same words; the form's own button reads `Create` (or `Save` where it changes something), and while the form is open the opener reads `Cancel` (rulings of 2026-09-20). A control whose face is a glyph carries the table's words as its `aria-label` and `title`. Secondary phrasing inside a sentence stays.

| Verb     | What it does                                                                                             | Objects                                                                                                                   |
| -------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| New      | creates a record                                                                                         | New claim · New cycle · New reflection · New consolidation · New section · New citation · New member · New key · New file |
| Start    | begins a workflow step or a run                                                                          | Start next step · Start dispatch                                                                                          |
| Pause    | stops new assignments and leaves live leases alone; never the accent                                     | Pause dispatch                                                                                                            |
| Claim    | takes a review                                                                                           | Claim review                                                                                                              |
| Submit   | sends a verdict                                                                                          | Submit verdict                                                                                                            |
| Halt     | stops leases, behind a guard                                                                             | Halt lease · Halt all leases                                                                                              |
| Edit     | changes a field                                                                                          | Edit standing · Edit introduction · Edit role · Edit citation · Edit `<section name>`                                     |
| Extend   | lengthens a machine's lease (`sandbox.extend`); a session lease has no extend and never grows the button | Extend lease                                                                                                              |
| Merge    | carries reviewed code to main; added 2026-09-22 for the publication a signed-in operator merges          | Merge reviewed proposal                                                                                                   |
| Release  | gives a machine back                                                                                     | Release machine                                                                                                           |
| Download | takes a copy of a file — a browser action, not a record mutation, which is why it is here at all         | Download file                                                                                                             |

A label outside the table is not an action and carries no verb from it: the filters (`Mine`, `Everyone`, a state word, `Clear filters`), a form's `Create` and `Save`, `Cancel` and `Close`, pagination, a view toggle (`Show published version`), a fold that only opens a panel (`Operations`), a control that only re-reads what is already on screen (`Load latest claim`, `Refresh download link`, `Check references`), and a link that only navigates (`Open record →`). Two administrative controls still have no verb the table names — a membership's `Remove` and a key's `Replace key…` / `Revoke key and descendants` — and keep their own words until the vocabulary grows a verb for taking access away.

Naming follows the same rule of one word per thing. `The record` is the map's object graph and only that; `Details` is the last block of every record page; the inventory of everything the project holds is **Knowledge**, not `Records`, which said the same word three times; and retained files are **Files** under Research, beside the Knowledge they belong to, so the Feed section holds the feed and nothing else.

## Remote rows (2026-09-17)

A service outside this process can own rows in the sidebar. It publishes a manifest (packages/contracts/src/ui-manifest.ts) saying what each row holds; a plugin here (packages/sandboxes first) registers the rows and proxies their reads; the browser renders them through the `collection` and `record` view kinds (views/remote.tsx) in the same list and record anatomy as everything else. The manifest never says how a thing looks: columns are Merv's types, the liveness phrase and attention reason use the same modules as sessions, identifiers appear only as machine text in Details, and Act holds only controls bound to tools this process registers. The first row is Sandboxes under Agents.

## No explanatory text (founder ruling, 2026-09-17)

The founder, on the Code sketch: "too much text on the code page. Get rid of the text that describes things. Anything that is not actually giving useful information about something needs to go." A sentence whose subject is the page, the visualisation or the mechanism is chrome: no legends, no captions explaining what a dot or a line means, no section ledes saying what the section is, no subtitles about where data comes from, no help text under controls, no empty-state hints that explain the system. What stays is information about the record: names, state words, counts, timestamps, the server's own gate sentence in Act, error lines, field labels. A mixed sentence keeps only the clause that is a fact about the record.

## Structure over prose (founder ruling, 2026-09-17)

The founder, on the Work page and an experiment record: "we have workflows, so we can always just show the next gate of the workflow. Remove the ids of attempts or other things; we don't need them to clog the visuals."

- **Act is a ladder, not a paragraph.** Every record with a workflow instance shows its program's own states in order, each marked _done_, _here_ or _next_, with the time it was entered; the one control that acts stands beside it. The rungs and the marks come from `workflow.process`, which the row's own `ui.read` serves with the record (`packages/experiments/src/ui.ts`, `packages/tasks/src/ui.ts`), so a record page never asks a second time for its gate. A blocker is printed only where its next move is a person's (founder, 2026-09-22), and then never in the server's words: exactly the Code codes `code_publication_pending`, `code_publication_stale`, `code_publication_disabled`, `code_publication_incident`, `code_publish_unverifiable`, `code_base_admission`, `code_quarantined`, `code_merge_conflict` while its resolution task is suspended, and `code_base_pending` about main, each read as one human sentence made from that blocker's own facts, in the voice of ruling 11, with who ends the wait, and a control only where a page of this app makes the move (`packages/ui/web/views/code-blockers.ts`). Every other code stays unprinted: it is an instruction to the agent holding the tool, and the node's state word and the ladder already say what a person can see. The server's own instruction is kept folded behind `Agent instructions`, as ruling 11 keeps it. Unsettled prerequisites keep their names and state words.
- **No numbers that name a thing.** Attempt, round, revision and request numbers leave the pages entirely, in list rows, standing lines, history, details and property cards alike; they stay in routes and in the arguments of controls. A directory name that is only an identifier names nobody, so the page says nothing rather than printing it.
- **Absence renders nothing.** No "no result attached", "not reviewed", "no outcome recorded" or "no conclusion recorded yet": a section, a clause or a cell with nothing in it is not drawn.
- **One line per round.** A review round reads as the gate it was read at, the word it came back with, and when — and is the link to its own verdict.
- **The paper is sections, not a toolbar.** The paper has no Act block: each section's `Edit <section name>` sits beside its own heading, `New section` beside its document's, and the citation controls in the References block. The skeleton of four documents renders whether or not anything is written yet, Problem & scope first. A file record likewise has no Act: `Download file` sits with the file it copies.
- **Home leads with what is running.** History: superseded on 2026-09-20 and again on 2026-10-05.

## One read a page (founder ruling, 2026-09-17, on load time)

Home took twenty-odd requests and five to ten seconds. A page is now one read.

- **`ui.home`** composes everything both home pages draw — the project, the actors, the gate of every workflow instance, and the records of every row that declares a `home` part (its list tool and the fields kept, under the row's id; a record id in prose opens at that row's path) — by calling read-only tools inside one snapshot scope (`packages/ui/src/home.ts`). The shell names no record kind: Tasks, Experiments, Reviews, Cycles and Reflections declare their parts in their own `ui.ts`, and @merv/research registers the Work row in the `lead` group, which stands above Now and is where `/` opens. A part whose answer this caller may not read is null: the page draws what it has, and a row that cannot load renders nothing rather than an error with a code in it.
- **`ui.shell`** answers who is asking and which project is open alongside its rows, so opening the app is `/auth/config`, `/account` and one scoped read instead of five sequential requests.
- **The browser asks once.** `useTool` shares one in-flight request per answer, so the rail and the page it frames never ask the same question twice; a reload always asks again. The rail's needs-you count, the map and the standing line all read `ui.home`.
- **Now** is the standing line and three lists — needs you, with an agent, waiting — and nothing else: the Introduction belongs to the paper, and what is merely running is on Home.
- **The Act slot carries no heading**: the ladder and its one control are their own sentence. A claim is its statement, its state word and how many experiments test it; its scope, evidence and history open with it. Nobody is named by an identifier: a directory name that is an id names nobody, and the rail's account row falls back to the signed-in address, then to initials.

## Rulings of 2026-09-20 (the audit)

The owner audited every page and asked for all of it to be fixed, under one standing instruction: the UI must be simple, and it says things with elements — a glyph, a pill, a control, where a thing sits — before it says them with text. Where a ruling below contradicts an older one above, this one wins; the older text is marked where it stood.

1. **Everything that is read passes AA.** Every text token and every kind label holds at least 4.5:1 on `--bg`, `--bg-elev` and `--bg-soft` in both themes, the 11px uppercase labels included. The values are the ones in the token blocks of `styles.css`.

   | Token           | Light     | Dark      |
   | --------------- | --------- | --------- |
   | `--text`        | `#111827` | `#e5e7eb` |

   | `--faint`       | `#666d7b` | `#8e8e98` |
   | `--supports`    | `#1f7a3a` | `#5fc46e` |
   | `--qualifies`   | `#946000` | `#d4a045` |

   | `--kind-purple` | `#6d28d9` | `#a78bfa` |
   | `--kind-blue`   | `#2563eb` | `#60a5fa` |
   | `--kind-teal`   | `#0f766e` | `#14b8a6` |

   What is drawn but never read — a separator dot, the em dash of an absent fact, a disabled glyph, the map's edges — takes `--ghost` (`#9ca3af` / `#71717a`), so nothing legible has to be that pale. The primary button's ground is `--active-fill`, `#2563eb` in both themes, because white type has to stay legible on it when `--active` lifts for the dark ground. The `KIND` table holds `--kind-*` tokens, never values, and a placeholder is read, so it takes `--faint`.

2. **Nothing a pointer lands on is under 28px, and nothing a finger lands on is under 40px.** `--hit` is 28px, and 40px under `pointer: coarse`. Chips, segments, tabs, the search field, glyph buttons, a record picker's field, and the words and cite boxes a delivery or a verdict is written with measure themselves by it. A quiet control stays as small as it reads and grows an invisible target around itself (`.btn-text`, `.btn--sm`, a disclosure summary), so the page does not move. A link that stands on its own is such a control — the way back, `Open the review`, a Now card's name, an attached file, the verdict on a row, `Open record` — and a list row is one target from edge to edge: where its name is the link because the row carries a second one, the link reaches over the whole row and a press on the row's second line is handed to it. An inline link inside a sentence is text and stays text. Under `pointer: coarse` every field is 40px tall and set at 16px — a select and a bare field inside a form included, whether or not it wears a class of its own; `Field` and `Area` put the class on what they render — because a phone zooms the page to a smaller one the moment it is touched.

3. **Glyphs stand where they replace words.** This extends "Icons only in the rail". Every glyph is one entry in `icons.tsx`, the same thin line on the same grid, drawn by one `Icon`. They appear where they replace words and never as decoration: the rail; an empty state; a control whose face is a glyph (edit, add, close, copy, view source, open, bring a file from disk, the pager), which always carries the verb table's words as `aria-label` and `title`; the arrow that ends a control or link which navigates, and the caret of a fold or a menu — every `<details>` opens from the one `Summary` component, which draws that caret and turns it, and the browser's own triangle is never drawn; the check beside what is chosen; the type of a file, in the file list, the document head and an attached file; and the gutter of a feed line. Kind labels, page titles, map cards and record headers still show a word in a colour and no glyph.

4. **Filters are told apart by shape.** Tabs switch which of several lists the page shows, on a hairline strip that runs the width of the page over the control row, with an ink underline on the one in force; when the lists are kinds of one collection the first tab is `All`, a tab with nothing under it is not drawn, and with one kind there are no tabs. Segments switch between two readings of one list (`Mine` / `Everyone`), one of which always holds. Chips are filters, each a soft pill once pressed, with its count beside its word. What is pressed is outlined in `--faint`, so the state has one cue at 3:1 where the fill alone is a step off the ground. A number beside a word is the number of rows pressing it would show: chips and tabs count the rows the other controls keep, and which chips and tabs there are is read from the whole list, so they stand still. All three are pressed buttons in a named group, and none of them wears the accent. The search field is a glyph and the one word `Search`; what it reads is its accessible name.

5. **A list says its kind only when kinds mix.** A row's kind label is printed only while the rows on screen are of more than one kind; a word repeated down a whole list says nothing.

6. **An empty place carries its own first step.** One `EmptyState` everywhere: the kind's glyph on a wash of the kind's colour, a few words, and — where the reader may make the first record — the creation control itself, which is then the page's one primary; the control row takes it back once there are rows. No hint explains the system. A failed load wears the same shape in the refusal colour, filtered-to-nothing says `Nothing matches`, or `Nothing here is yours` only where `Mine` is pressed over a list that holds nothing of the reader's at all (a search or a chip that emptied the reader's own rows did not make them anyone else's) — never a sentence built round the list's noun — and offers `Clear filters`, and a section that can only be empty is its name and a zero. Where the state is the whole page (`Page not found`) its words are the page's h1.

7. **One creation form.** A form opens under the control row, held to 640px with its fields full width and a select at its natural width. The cursor goes to its first field; Escape means Cancel wherever the focus is, and neither Escape nor the opener's `Cancel` can take a form away while its request is in flight or kept for a retry — a form locks by disabling the fieldset round its fields, and the box it opened in reads that lock; the cursor returns to the opener, and follows it from the empty state to the control row when the first record lands; the form closes after success. A desk on a record's page (a delivery, a verdict) marks itself `data-draft` while it holds anything unsent or its request is in flight, and the split pane's Escape then stays on the record. A field still empty holds the button and says nothing; only a limit that was overrun is said. The opener and the form's heading say what is being made, the button says `Create` or `Save`, and while the form is open the opener reads `Cancel` and drops the accent. A form that stands on its own (an editor beside a heading, the key form) has the same manners through `OpenedForm`. This supersedes "opener, heading and button read the same words". Nobody types an identifier: a field that holds records is a record picker — search by name, chips for what is chosen, ids handed to the tool — it locks with the form it stands in, and a list with nothing to choose from says so (`No files of yours yet`) apart from a search that matched nothing (`No matches`). Two fields take an identifier because whether it names anything is the question: `Check references` reads what an agent cited, pasted as it was written, and `New member` takes the account ID a person was shown and handed over (the server lists no accounts to pick from); each says so in its placeholder.

8. **Markdown is read as a document.** Briefs, deliveries, reports, post bodies, paper sections, conclusions and the introduction render through one parser and one renderer (`markdown.tsx`): nothing is handed to the browser as HTML, an image loads nothing, and only `http`, `https`, `mailto` and relative addresses become links, a root-relative one through the router. The body sans, a 72ch measure, ruled tables that scroll inside their own block, code on `--bg-soft`; a Markdown file's head has one glyph control that shows its source. A link holds no link — inside a label an address stays text and a link or an image is only the words it reads as, so a badge opens what its link names. The parser's work is bounded per character (code spans and brackets are found once per run of text, no pattern tries a stretch of spaces two ways), and a text over 200,000 characters is shown as its source rather than read. A record id written in prose reads as the record's name and link, named from `artifact.list` and `ui.home` and read only when the text mentions one; an id no list names is its short form with the whole id in the hover title. A file says its type as a glyph and a human word (`Markdown`, `JSON`, `PNG image`), never as a media type, and a hash is its first twelve characters with a copy control.

9. **Time is written for a person.** An absolute time is the reader's locale to the minute (`Sep 20, 2026, 10:38 AM`) inside a `<time>` whose title is the ISO string; a relative time is a `<time>` whose title is the absolute one. Details ends with Created and Updated through `timeRows`, and Updated is left out when it reads the same, to the minute, as Created.

10. **The task page is one section, Checks.** A check is stated once, in one box: its number and text, the producer's line (a Met / Not met pill and the note), the reviewer's line (the finding pill and the finding), and the files either cites, each opening once in place. Before a delivery a row is the check alone. The verdict pill, the review's sentence and the way to the review stand once, in that review's post in the thread under History (ruling 3 of 2026-10-02), and not above the rows as well. Under the rows `Files` holds the brief as a collapsed disclosure — a brief somebody wrote is open until something is delivered; the one the server composes from the title, the goal and the checks stays shut under the word `Brief`, because the page has already said all of it and it ends in instructions to an agent. What was delivered, in every round, opens from its own post in the thread and from each check that cites it, and is not listed under `Files` again. There is no delivery desk: a task's delivery names its worker's own `code.commit`, which only a leased worker can make, so no browser can deliver. A disclosure that has said a file's title does not say it again in the head it opens. Waits on and Unblocks are one line a record: kind label, linked name, state pill. The verdict page uses the same rows under the same title, `Checks`, is titled by what it judged (the kind label already says Review; an experiment's gate, `Design` or `Results`, opens its standing line), and no longer lists a file a row already opens. Its desk is held to 640px, `Synopsis` under a label of its own, its count shown only as the limit nears, and the notes a row opens held to that width too. Every record page has one header anatomy — state pill beside the title, one muted sentence under it; every state a program can stand in has a tone, a review gate the amber of work in flight, and `resolved` / `missing` read green / red — and a row's review clause adds only what that pill cannot say (`unclaimed`, `with <name>`, the verdict), never `in review` a second time; and what a record's author wrote at length is its own `Description` section above the kind's content. A cycle's page says its relations the same way (`Waits on`, `Unblocks`), and a step its gate already refuses for want of the definition is not offered as a button that can only fail: the move is `Write the definition →`, to the paper where the definition is written.

11. **The standing line speaks to the person.** This supersedes "the server's own instruction verbatim". A Now card reads, in order: kind label, the record's name as a link, one human sentence derived from the gate's facts (`Submit the design for review`, `Review this delivery`, `With <name>`, `Waiting on A, B and 2 more`), who and when, and one control level with the name only where a page of this app makes the move: `Claim review` in place where the gate says `review.start` is ready, or the control another plugin's blocker names. A move nothing here can make (an experiment's design or results go through an agent) has no control: the name is already the way to the record, and an accent that repeated it would promise an act. The server's instruction is kept, folded behind `Agent instructions`, for the agent it was written for. The ordering policy keeps its four codes, with one addition: a gate that reports no blocker is simply running unless it holds the reader's own move open — work a review sent back, or work never begun, reports only `begin` as ready while its submission already waits — and then it is the reader's, and work sent back says so (`Changes requested: submit the design for review`). A task's delivery is never a person's move: only its leased worker can make one. Work an agent began stays that agent's. A record out for review is listed once, as its review; a gate the page cannot read carries the server's own reason as its sentence rather than the bare word `Waiting`, and the fold does not repeat it. With nothing to do the page says `Nothing needs you` under a check.

12. **Home says each thing once, in plain words.** History: this described the map Home retired on 2026-10-02; what still holds is that counts agree with their numbers (`1 needs you`, `2 need you`) and a stat is a 22px number over an 11px label.

13. **Sessions carries state in elements.** Dispatch is one line: its name, a status pill, who last set it, and one control — `Start dispatch` wears the accent and `Pause dispatch` never does. `Halt all leases` stands beside the Leases heading, outlined in the refusal colour, and its guard is still where consequences are named. Counts sit beside their headings, and `Agents` counts what Home counts, with the retired ones named apart (`2 · 1 retired`); work is named without its `Work:` / `Review:` prefix and a lens by its words; revision numbers are gone; a time in the agent panel is relative, with the moment in its title. Under 720px every ruled list stacks, each fact under its column's name. A reflection's perspectives and a consolidation's decisions are ruled lists too. The agent panel closes with a glyph and its scope switch is segments; choosing an agent brings the panel's head to the top of the view, again once it has loaded and has its height, and puts the cursor on its name. Work ready to assign is named by a link as tall as a target.

14. **The feed and Code.** A post is an initials disc, its author and a time over a Markdown body; an event line carries a small glyph in the same gutter, so text has one left edge and times one right edge. Code without GitHub is one empty state with the way to Settings › Integrations; connected and empty is `Branches 0` and `Pull requests 0`.

15. **Settings, the rail and an address nothing answers.** Every room of Settings stands under the one `Settings` title, with buttons of one size. Members and Keys belong to a person's account, so a session opened with a bearer credential is not offered them, and whoever arrives at one by its address is told what would open it, with `Sign out` as the one step. The plugin table and the sidebar-row table are collapsed folds — name, count, one health pill — that open by themselves only when something is unwell, with the unwell entries first; their rows are the ruled list Sessions uses, which stacks under 720px, so how an entry stands is never a column a phone scrolls away. A rail heading is left out when its single row says the same word, the current row says `aria-current`, and the account row is one button whose second line appears only where the role adds to the name; it opens a menu that is operated as one — the cursor goes to its first item, the arrows, Home and End move between items, Escape hands the cursor back, Tab leaves and shuts it. The document is titled by the page's own h1 (`Work · <project> · Merv`). A mistyped address is `Page not found` and a way home; a plugin is mentioned only when the address belongs to a view this build draws and the lifecycle table holds that view's plugin in a state other than active.

16. **JSON is read as a tree.** A JSON file opens as folds (`json-view.tsx`), not as a page of braces: the first two levels stand open, anything deeper is its key and a quiet count (`{ 12 }`, `[ 258 ]`), and what is closed is not rendered at all; an object or an array past a hundred entries is drawn a hundred at a time behind `Show 100 more`, so a two-megabyte file opens as fast as a small one. A fold is a real button — the caret of `icons.tsx` and the key itself, a target tall, with a truthful `aria-expanded` — and an Alt-press opens or closes everything beneath it; the root has no key, so its count is what its toggle stands beside. Keys, numbers, `true`, `false` and `null` are machine text in the mono, a string is somebody's words in the sans between quotes, and each kind has an ink of its own from the tokens (`--muted` keys, `--text` strings, `--kind-teal` numbers, `--kind-purple` booleans, `--faint` for `null`, counts and an array's places), every one 4.5:1 on all three grounds and none of them the only cue. Depth is the indent and nothing else — no guide line, no edge — and a tree too deep for a phone scrolls inside its own block. A string is text, with three exceptions that are each the whole of the string: a record id is the record's name and link, as in a document; an `http` or `https` address opens in another tab; a commit or a digest (40 or 64 hex digits) is its first eight and last six in the mono with all of it in the hover title and a copy control. Nothing else is ever a link and nothing is handed to the browser as HTML. A string past 120 characters is cut behind `Show all`. The glyph control on the document's head shows the source, as it does for Markdown; JSON that does not parse is left exactly as it was written, and then has no such control, because its source is already what is shown.

Every change passes `npm run typecheck:ui`, `npm run test:ui`, `npm run build:ui` and prettier, in both themes and at 390px.

## Running (founder ruling, 2026-09-25)

## Home, and the agent live (owner decisions, 2026-10-05)

1. **Home is the project's pulse.** `/` opens Home and its row leads the rail, with Needs you's count; Work and the paper follow it. Its heading is the project's name, with the cycle the project is on under it: the cycle's name, its state, how many of the work items it names are done, and `Open Work →`. Under that, Needs you (what was the Now page; `/now` opens Home), then Live now — each agent at work with its unit, its role and what it is doing at this moment, the way to its unit's sidebar on Work — then Latest: the newest verdicts, files and paper revisions, each the way to its record. Each part reads an answer the app already asks for and fails on its own line, never the page's. An address nothing answers offers `Home`; a record's way back is still Work.
2. **A unit's agent is live in its sidebar** (operators only). The sidebar's Agent section lists the unit's sessions as chips, newest first, the live one chosen, each saying what it continues; under them the chosen session's stream: what it says, its thinking folded to its first line, each tool call with its arguments and what it answered (a failure says so), and its milestones as quiet dividers. It follows the newest line while the reader is at its foot and offers `Jump to latest` otherwise; it draws the newest few hundred blocks, with earlier ones a press away; and it closes while the sidebar is shut or the tab hidden, reopening from the last line it holds.
